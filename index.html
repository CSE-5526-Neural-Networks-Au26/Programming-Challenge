02 The Task

Each clip is one speaker expressing one of six emotions. You are given a labelled training set and a labelled public test set; your job is to train a model that generalizes to unseen speakers. The data is split by speaker identity — no speaker appears in more than one split — which makes this much harder than a random split and is where most of the difficulty (and the fun) lives.

You may change anything: architecture, features, and training strategy. Common directions:

Architecture

  • Bidirectional LSTM
  • CNN front-end for local features
  • Attention over LSTM outputs
  • Transformer encoder
  • Hybrid CNN + LSTM / Transformer

Features

  • Mel-spectrogram (baseline)
  • MFCCs + delta / delta-delta
  • Log-Mel filterbanks
  • Feature combinations

Training strategy

  • Dropout, weight decay, label smoothing
  • LR warmup or cosine annealing
  • Data augmentation (e.g. SpecAugment)

Rules

  • Python + PyTorch only
  • No pre-trained models
  • No external data
  • All code written by your team

04 Baseline

A deliberately simple 2-layer LSTM on 64-bin Mel-spectrograms. It scores weighted F1 = 0.4276 on the public test set — well above the ~0.167 chance level for six classes, with plenty of room to improve.

64-bin Mel-spec (25ms / 10ms)

05 Getting Started

Python and PyTorch only. You're encouraged to run experiments on OSC (Ohio Supercomputer Center) to avoid local compute delays — reach out anytime with setup questions.

1 · Install

pip install torch torchaudio scikit-learn pandas matplotlib wandb tqdm

2 · Data layout

dataset/
  train/  audio/  train_labels.csv
  test/   audio/  test_labels.csv

3 · Train the baseline

python train.py --data_dir dataset --results_dir results \
                --team_name baseline --run_name baseline_train

Splits train/validation 85/15, trains up to 100 epochs with early stopping, writes best_model.pt, norm_stats.pt, checkpoint.pt and loss_curve.png to results/<team>/, and logs to Weights & Biases. Re-running the command resumes from the checkpoint.

4 · Evaluate & make your CSV

python test.py --test_dir dataset/test --results_dir results \
               --team_name baseline --run_name baseline_test

Prints weighted and per-class F1, logs a confusion matrix, and writes your <team>.csv of predictions.

    Suggested reading

    Widely cited work on deep speech emotion recognition — inspiration, not required reading.

    Architectures & survey

    CNN-based

    Zhao, J., Mao, X., & Chen, L. (2019). Speech emotion recognition using deep 1D & 2D CNN LSTM networks. Biomedical Signal Processing and Control, 47, 312–323.

    Badshah, A. M., Ahmad, J., Rahim, N., & Baik, S. W. (2017). Speech emotion recognition from spectrograms with deep convolutional neural network. Int. Conf. on Platform Technology and Service.

    RNN-based

    Han, K., Yu, D., & Tashev, I. (2014). Speech emotion recognition using deep neural network and extreme learning machine. INTERSPEECH, 223–227.

    Lee, J., & Tashev, I. (2015). High-level feature representation using recurrent neural network for speech emotion recognition. INTERSPEECH.

    Attention-based

    Mirsamadi, S., Barsoum, E., & Zhang, C. (2017). Automatic speech emotion recognition using recurrent neural networks with local attention. ICASSP.

    Zhao, Z., Bao, Z., Zhang, Z., Schuller, B., & Zheng, Y. (2021). Exploring deep spectrum representations via attention-based recurrent and convolutional neural networks for speech emotion recognition. IEEE Access.

    Survey

    Akçay, M. B., & Oğuz, K. (2020). Speech emotion recognition: emotional models, databases, features, preprocessing methods, supporting modalities, and classifiers. Speech Communication, 116, 56–76.

    Features & training strategy

    Spectrogram / Mel-spectrogram

    Allen, J. B. (1977). Short term spectral analysis, synthesis, and modification by discrete Fourier transform. IEEE Trans. ASSP, 25(3), 235–238. — Basis of the STFT and the spectrogram.

    MFCCs, delta & delta-delta

    Davis, S. B., & Mermelstein, P. (1980). Comparison of parametric representations for monosyllabic word recognition in continuously spoken sentences. IEEE Trans. ASSP, 28(4), 357–366. — MFCCs; delta and delta-delta as first and second temporal derivatives.

    Learning rate warmup

    Vaswani, A., et al. (2017). Attention is all you need. NeurIPS, 30. — Warmup + inverse-square-root decay schedule.

    Label smoothing

    Szegedy, C., Vanhoucke, V., Ioffe, S., Shlens, J., & Wojna, Z. (2016). Rethinking the inception architecture for computer vision. CVPR, 2818–2826.

    Audio data augmentation

    Park, D. S., et al. (2019). SpecAugment: a simple data augmentation method for automatic speech recognition. INTERSPEECH, 2613–2617. — Time and frequency masking on the log-Mel spectrogram.
