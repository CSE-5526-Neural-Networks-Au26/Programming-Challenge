02 The Task

Each clip is one speaker expressing one of six emotions. You are given a labelled training set and a labelled public test set; your job is to train a model that generalizes to unseen speakers. The data is split by speaker identity — no speaker appears in more than one split — which makes this much harder than a random split and is where most of the difficulty (and the fun) lives.

You may change anything: architecture, features, and training strategy. Common directions:

Architecture

  • Bidirectional LSTM
  • CNN front-end for local features
  • Attention over LSTM outputs
  • Transformer encoder
  • Hybrid CNN + LSTM / Transformer

Features

  • Mel-spectrogram (baseline)
  • MFCCs + delta / delta-delta
  • Log-Mel filterbanks
  • Feature combinations

Training strategy

  • Dropout, weight decay, label smoothing
  • LR warmup or cosine annealing
  • Data augmentation (e.g. SpecAugment)

Rules

  • Python + PyTorch only
  • No pre-trained models
  • No external data
  • All code written by your team

04 Baseline

A deliberately simple 2-layer LSTM on 64-bin Mel-spectrograms. It scores weighted F1 = 0.4276 on the public test set — well above the ~0.167 chance level for six classes, with plenty of room to improve.

05 Get on the Leaderboard

The leaderboard scores your predictions server-side against the public test labels — no self-reported numbers. To appear:

  1. Train your model, then run test.py to generate <team_name>.csv (columns clip_id,predicted_emotion).
  2. Add your CSV to the course repo under submissions/<team_name>.csv.
  3. A GitHub Action scores it and updates this page automatically within a minute or two.

Suggested reading

Surveys & features

Akçay & Oğuz (2020), Speech Communication — full SER pipeline survey.

Davis & Mermelstein (1980) — MFCCs; delta / delta-delta as temporal derivatives.

CNN-based

Zhao, Mao & Chen (2019) — 1D/2D CNN-LSTM. Badshah et al. (2017) — CNN on spectrograms as images.

RNN & attention

Han, Yu & Tashev (2014); Lee & Tashev (2015) — DNN/BiLSTM features. Mirsamadi et al. (2017) — local attention over LSTM.

Training

Vaswani et al. (2017) — warmup schedule. Szegedy et al. (2016) — label smoothing. Park et al. (2019) — SpecAugment.
